Independent spot-check of the commit-response report
2026-10-20. Original report, data, source, and manifest left unchanged.

Verdict

The main empirical result survives this check: there is no detected prompt
increase in the primary outcome under the chosen rotation null, and the
dominant UA cohort did not request any current commit within an hour of its
reconstructed Forgejo publication. Several statistical and prose claims
need correction before publication. These do not reverse the main result.

Scope of verification

- All 53 entries in the supplied MANIFEST.sha256 passed.
- Independently summed minute windows from the frozen exported table:
  40 episodes, 659 pre-window requests, 829 post-window requests.
- Independently constructed the 60-rotation block contributions and 10,000
  draws: p=0.1513848615138486, matching the report. Re-running the supplied
  rotation implementation also reproduced primary-null.npy bit for bit.
- Independently resolved commit prefixes against the publication table and
  recounted primary-cohort GETs: 90,122. The earliest request-minute start
  was 1.3461 hours after publication. Thus minute rounding cannot explain
  away the absence of sub-hour requests in this cohort.
- Verified old-hash status counts from hash-requests.csv.gz and the snapshot.
- Re-ran the five deterministic tests for window indexing, rotations,
  eligibility, Holm/Kaplan-Meier, and clustering; all passed.
- Inspected publication reconstruction, aggregation, analysis, power code,
  amendments, and outputs. Did not re-read the private VPS logs, rerun the
  remote aggregation, re-identify addresses, or independently re-audit all
  GitHub events. The claims about exact hourly agreement and remote
  reproduction remain the other thread's checks, not new independent ones.

Corrections and qualifications, in order of practical importance

1. Limit the conclusion to observed behavior; no general exclusion of monitoring.

REPORT.md:8-11, 35-53, 439-467 and the pasted summary.
The observed absence of prompt hash-specific GETs is substantial evidence
against a monitor that promptly FETCHED EACH NEW COMMIT in this cohort and
this logging window. It cannot exclude polling that did not fetch commits,
infrequent monitoring, long delays, other UAs, another host, or mechanisms
outside these logs. Branch-view timing is consistent with link traversal;
it does not identify the mechanism uniquely. "Fast fetches ... were not
monitoring" is stronger than the available observational evidence.

Suggested conclusion:
"We found no convincing evidence of repeated, prompt fetching after pushes.
The dominant user-agent cohort mostly revisited older commits and made no
commit-specific GET within an hour of publication. This argues against a
crawler that promptly fetched every new commit, while leaving intermittent
or otherwise unobserved monitoring unresolved."

2. Separate raw pre/post differences from null-adjusted excess.

REPORT.md:13-17, 230-247; results/timing-results.json.
Raw mean post-minus-pre = (829-659)/40 = +4.25 requests per episode.
Expected difference over rotations = +1.8875.
Adjusted excess = +2.3625. The interval [-1.4684, +7.7504] is for that
adjusted excess, not for the raw after-minus-before difference.
Without Oct13 the RAW mean is +0.5526, while ADJUSTED excess is -0.4860
(p=0.7123, 38 episodes). The summary switches estimands between +2.4 and
+0.55. Label both or compare +2.36 to -0.49 consistently.

3. Detection simulations are conditional, not a blanket power guarantee.

source/power.py:27-41; REPORT.md:20-23 and 426-435.
The headline simulation injects a response after EVERY eligible episode,
including dates when this cohort was almost absent. It draws placebo
publication minutes under the rotation null and injects Poisson requests
in minutes +1..+10; "one per new commit" is an EXPECTED count in that
model, not exactly one request for each new hash. It tests volume, not
hash-specific behavior or a general family of monitoring mechanisms.

The supplied difficulty-2-only simulation already gives ~68% detection for
+10 requests per episode, versus 300/300 for the all-date simulation.
There are 20 eligible difficulty-2 episodes; the Oct6-13 active subset has
only 15 episodes on six dates. A 15-episode active-only power study has not
been supplied. My timing rerun on those 15 gives raw +11.2, adjusted +6.2733,
p=0.1532, so the main null remains.

Use "300/300 detections under the all-push injection model," rather than
suggesting guaranteed detection of a real monitor. Synthetic calibration
supports the mechanics under the simulated nulls; it does not demonstrate
exchangeability of the actual observational publication schedule.

4. The exact zero-event probability bounds require unsupported assumptions.

source/power.py:45-70; REPORT.md:439-443.
The numbers 6.7% and 18.1% use 1-0.05**(1/n), the zero-success bound under
independent Bernoulli opportunities with a common probability. The report
does not establish that model for clustered pushes and crawler campaigns;
early inactive dates also differ from active dates. They are not
assumption-free bounds on monitoring. Remove them or explicitly present
them as an illustrative binomial model. The 271-commit bound in power.json
is correctly named naive_independent but is likewise unsuitable for an
inferential claim because commits arrive in batches.

The 43-episode denominator is hard-coded in power.py. The first-fetch table
actually contains 48 study episodes, including five on the Anubis deployment
day; 43 excludes those five. Name that exclusion when quoting 0/43.

5. The "per novel commit" statistic is mislabeled / implemented differently.

source/analysis.py:224-245; REPORT.md sensitivity table.
The docstring promises sum(D_i)/sum(n_i), but the code computes
sum(n_i * D_i)/sum(n_i), where D_i is the episode request difference and
n_i is its novel-commit count. That is an episode difference weighted by
batch size, not aggregate requests per novel commit.

With 235 eligible novel commits, the promised aggregate per-commit
statistic is 170/235 = +0.7234 raw, +0.4021 adjusted. Its p-value remains
0.1514 because this only rescales the primary statistic by a fixed positive
constant. The reported +0.7702, p=0.5451 is for the different weighted
statistic. Relabel the implemented statistic, or implement the promised one;
do not interpret the current row as an independent per-commit result.

6. The stale-history summary overstates both classification and 404s.

REPORT.md:90-99; pasted summary "about 9,500 ... and got 404s".
Known hashes from removed history: 6,615 GETs, of which 4,960 were 404.
Unresolved hashes: 2,873 GETs, of which 2,132 were 404. These hashes have
not all been demonstrated to belong to removed history.
Combined: 9,488 GETs, 7,092 with status 404. Another 2,337 were 403.
The report body says "mostly 404," which is more accurate than the pasted
summary, but unresolved hashes should remain a separate category.

7. The interval's dependence assumptions need a broader sensitivity.

source/analysis.py:122-125; source/commitstats.py:clusters/cluster_bootstrap.
The published interval resamples 32 clusters formed only by overlapping
60-minute event windows. Nonoverlapping windows can still share a crawler
campaign or a day's activity. Those clusters are not established independent.

As a simple additional sensitivity, using the same per-episode adjusted
contributions but resampling whole UTC dates (10 clusters, 10,000 bootstrap
draws, seed 20261020) gives [-1.9666, +10.4654], wider than the published
[-1.4684, +7.7504]. This does not make the day-bootstrap interval a validated
95% guarantee with so few dates; it shows that dependence assumptions matter.
Neither interval changes the broad absence of a clear timing result.

Minor precision point

The hash-age table uses minute midpoints, not exact request timestamps.
The reported 87,270 requests older than seven days is reproducible under
that rule; 87,265 are definitely older than seven days even at the start
of their request minute. This tiny boundary difference is immaterial to
the result. State the binning precision rather than implying exact ages.

Recommended action

Retain the main negative finding, fix the metric labels and weighted row,
qualify power, remove or explicitly condition the binomial bounds, and
soften claims about mechanism. Preserve the useful distinction between
missing evidence of a statistical increase and direct observations of
what was fetched. No change to the essay or a prospective experiment was
made during this check.
